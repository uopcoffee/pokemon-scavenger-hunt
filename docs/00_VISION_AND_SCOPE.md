# 00. Vision and Scope

## Product statement

Luca's Creekside Region transforms a familiar cul-de-sac into a two-hour Pokémon campaign. Luca begins as a new Trainer, recruits a team, earns four Ranger Code fragments one chapter at a time while he helps Professor Oak investigate a pool anomaly and defeats a theatrical Team Rocket member, enters a secret Ranger vault, completes Victory Road, defeats the Champion, and then discovers Mew after everyone believes the adventure is over.

## Player and audience

- Primary player: Luca, age 7
- Supporting cast: family and participating neighbors
- Spectator group: immediate family only
- Play date: Saturday, August 1
- Planned start: 9:30 a.m.
- Professor Oak availability: 10:00 a.m. to noon
- Planned live game ending: approximately 11:30 a.m.
- Celebration: popsicles/treats and booster opening in the backyard after the story concludes

## Emotional arc

1. **Identity:** I am a real Pokémon Trainer.
2. **Delight:** My little sister is part of my world.
3. **Care:** Great Trainers help and protect Pokémon.
4. **Wonder:** Professor Oak's pool contains a real mystery.
5. **Excitement:** Team Rocket is physically blocking the mission.
6. **Mystery:** Four fragments unlock a secret place left by friends.
7. **Mastery:** Everything I learned is tested on Victory Road.
8. **Triumph:** I am Champion of the Creekside Region.
9. **Awe:** Mew appears only after the story seems finished.
10. **Belonging:** The Hall of Heroes recognizes everyone who helped.

## Design pillars

- Real-world play first
- Every reward is earned and narratively justified
- Adults perform recognizable characters with short scripts
- The app never asks Luca to stare at the screen for long
- Challenges are involved but forgiving
- The story uses callbacks: code fragments, the Sky Fragment, Oak's return visit, and the Mew signal
- The experience ends with family celebration, not immediately after the largest gift

## V2 scope

V2 is a complete design and implementation specification for:

- Seven numbered chapters
- One mid-game Professor Oak return checkpoint
- One fake Champion finale
- One Mew epilogue
- Four physical code fragments with no digital secret storage
- Adult-confirmed physical challenges
- Reward inventory and wrapping allocation
- Persistent progress and moderator recovery controls
- Hall of Heroes

## Out of scope for this iteration

- GPS or geofencing
- Camera verification
- Multiplayer accounts
- Real Pokémon battle rules
- Online services or databases
- Complex procedural mini-games
- Public display of private household information
