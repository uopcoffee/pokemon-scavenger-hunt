# Claude Design — Print Props Handoff (V7 story arc)

## What this is

The story arc for **Luca's Creekside Region** changed materially after you last
worked on the print pieces. This prompt gives you the new canonical arc and asks
you to bring the printed props in line with it.

The party is a single-player, one-morning Pokémon adventure for a seven-year-old.
The physical props are not decoration — several of them *are* the puzzle. Read
the fragment spec in section 4 carefully; it is the hardest and most important
piece of this handoff.

## 1. Do this first, before designing anything

**Deliverable 1 — a reconciliation table.** Inventory every print piece you have
already produced and classify each one against the new arc:

| Piece | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| … | KEEP / REVISE / RETIRE / — | one line |

Then list **NEW** pieces the new arc requires that you have not made.

Do not start redesigning until I have seen that table. If a piece you built is
now story-dead, say so plainly rather than trying to repurpose it.

## 2. What changed in the story

**The four fragments are now one object.** There used to be four separate "Ranger
Code fragments" and, separately, a "Sky Fragment / Mega Stone" prop from the pool.
Those are now the same thing: **four broken pieces of a single Sky Stone** that
fell over Creekside. Rangers Hannah and Noa smashed it apart so Team Rocket could
never take it whole.

**Chapter 1 gives Luca no card.** The blank four-slot "Ranger Code Record Card"
and pencil are **retired entirely**. There is no such prop. He also does not
receive a deck box.

**Where the pieces come from:**

| # | Chapter | Symbol | Handed over by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fairy Garden | Leaf | Nina / Auntie Ariel |
| 2 | Professor Oak's pool | Wave | The Partner Professors |
| 3 | Pokémon Center | Heart | Nurse Joy |
| 4 | Team Rocket Base | Flame | Mike |

Note the symbol changes: slot 2 was **Star**, now **Wave**. Slot 4 was **Wave**,
now **Flame**. Any existing art keyed to the old mapping is wrong.

**Nobody explains the pieces until Chapter 5.** Luca carries fragment 1 from the
garden knowing only the words "Sky Fragment." Mike delivers the reveal: they are
one stone, the numbers on them are a combination, and the Rangers hid their vault
at the closest place to the sky in their yard. Mike does **not** reveal the order.

**The vault is a locked box, not a door.** There is no keypad and no house entry.
It is a box on a **luggage combination lock**, staged at the Rangers' backyard
**trampoline**. In Chapter 6 Luca finds it and *fails* to open it — that failure
is the intended beat. The lid sends him to Professor Oak.

**Professor Oak solves it.** At the return checkpoint Oak guides Luca through
joining the jagged pieces, flipping to confirm the Sky Stone, reading the numbers,
and dialing the lock himself. The vault's whole contents open there.

**Victory Road is now a made-up trick-shot game** ("POKÉ", a HORSE variant) that
Auntie Ariel invents live off the backyard play equipment. The four fixed
stations — Tall Grass, Forest Crossing, ball target, Energy Tokens — no longer
exist, and neither does their signage.

**The Champion match is trivia and thanks**, not a target toss: three Pokémon
trivia questions, then Patrick names every helper present and Luca says what each
one did, then one question about his favourite teammate.

**After the credits**, Patrick asks Luca whether he still remembers the code.
Luca reads the four numbers off the assembled stone, and that triggers the Mew
epilogue.

## 4. The Sky Fragments — full spec

This prop carries the puzzle. Please treat it as an engineering problem, not just
an art problem.

### Behaviour required

1. Four pieces, cut with **jagged interlocking edges that join in exactly one
   arrangement**. Avoid regular or symmetric zigzags — a seven-year-old should be
   able to find the fit by feel, but there must be only one fit.
2. **Front face of each piece**: its symbol (Leaf / Wave / Heart / Flame) and one
   digit. The four fronts must NOT combine into a picture — no visual cue to the
   correct order from the front. On their own, the four numbers must look
   meaningless.
3. **Back face**: when the four are correctly joined and turned over, the backs
   form **one complete Sky Stone image**. This is the confirmation beat — the
   moment Luca sees he got it right.
4. Turned face up again and read **left to right**, the four digits give the lock
   combination in order.

### Two constraints that will ruin the prop if missed

**Mirroring.** Flipping a joined set reverses left-to-right order. The front
digit sequence and the back image must be designed as a **mirrored pair** so that
the arrangement which produces the correct Sky Stone on the back also produces
the correct digit order on the front. Please mock this up physically — on paper,
with scissors — before committing to final art.

**No printed digits.** The four digits are the real combination to a real lock
and must never be printed, typeset, filed, or transmitted. Design each front with
a **clean hand-lettering field beside the symbol**, sized for an adult to write
one digit in permanent marker. Leave it blank in every file you produce. Do not
put placeholder digits in the artwork, and do not ask me what the numbers are.

### Production notes

Deliver as a single sheet showing the assembled stone with the cut path marked,
so it prints once and cuts apart, rather than four separately registered pieces.
Double-sided registration on jagged shapes is unforgiving — print, laminate, then
cut. Assume outdoor handling, a pool deck, and a trampoline.

## 5. Hard constraints

- **Never print, store, or request the four digits.** Hand-lettering fields only.
- **Do not spoil the arc.** Nothing printed before Chapter 5 may hint that the
  fragments are one object, that they are a combination, or that a vault exists.
  This applies to the fragments themselves.
- **Preserve the existing visual identity.** Energetic glass-card, Pokémon-type
  energy field, trainer-card aesthetic. Evolve it; do not rebrand.
- **Outdoor readability** in August daylight, at a child's arm's length.
- **No surnames, addresses, phone numbers, or household details** on any prop.
- **Do not design the cast Quick Cards.** Those are generated from the repo's cast
  portal (`cast/`) and printed to PDF from the browser. They are handled.
