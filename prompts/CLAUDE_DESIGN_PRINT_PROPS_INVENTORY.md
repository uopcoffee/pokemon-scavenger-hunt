# Claude Design — Print Props Inventory Request

## What I need

A complete, honest inventory of every **printed physical prop** you have designed
for Luca's Creekside Region, and the reasoning behind each one.

**This is a read-and-report task only.** Do not design anything. Do not improve,
revise, consolidate, or propose changes to any piece. Do not ask what I plan to
do with this. I am going to review your answer before deciding anything, and a
clean picture of the current state is more useful to me than suggestions.

If you are unsure whether something counts, include it and say you were unsure.
Over-inclusion is fine here; omission is not.

## Scope

**In scope:** anything intended to be printed, cut, laminated, worn, or physically
handed to a person. Cards, tokens, labels, signs, stickers, badges, documents,
markers, wearables, packaging, tags, banners, scorecards.

**Out of scope:** app screens, web layouts, and the cast Quick Card pages that
render from the repository's cast portal.

Include pieces at **any** stage: finished, drafted, sketched, or abandoned.

## For every piece, tell me

1. **Name** — what you call it.
2. **What it physically is** — one sentence. Card, sign, sticker, token, folded
   document, and so on.
3. **Exact copy** — every word printed on it, verbatim, front and back. If a
   field is meant to be filled in by hand, say so and describe the field. If
   there is no text, say "no text."
4. **Dimensions, material, and finish** — as designed. If not yet decided, say
   "undecided."
5. **Quantity** needed.
6. **Where it appears** — which chapter, which character hands it over or stages
   it, and at what moment.
7. **What it is for** — the job it does in the morning. Does it carry
   information, gate a puzzle, mark a location, reward the player, or organize
   the adults?
8. **Why you designed it this way** — the reasoning. What you were solving for,
   what you tried and rejected, and any constraint that shaped it (child's
   reading level, outdoor light, one-handed handling, cost, print method).
9. **What it assumes about the story** — this one matters most to me. Name any
   plot point, character, location, sequence, or mechanic the piece depends on.
   For example: "assumes X is handed over blank," "assumes the reveal happens at
   Y," "assumes there are four of these," "assumes it is opened at Z."
10. **Status** — finished / draft / sketch / abandoned.
11. **File or artboard location**, if it exists.

## Also tell me

- **Dependencies between pieces.** Which props only make sense alongside another
  prop, and which are part of a matched set.
- **Anything you designed and then dropped**, and why you dropped it.
- **Anything you believe is needed but have not built**, with the reason it is
  still outstanding.
- **Anything you were never given enough information to finish.** Name the
  specific open question.
- **Any piece you are least confident about** — where you had to guess at intent,
  or where you suspect the design and the story may have drifted apart.

## Format

Group by chapter in story order, then a final section for pieces that span the
whole morning (packaging labels, badges, wearables, signage).

Use one block per piece with the eleven fields above as labelled lines. Do not
compress several props into a single entry, even when they are a set — list each
one, then note the set relationship under dependencies.

Prose summaries are not useful to me here. I need the itemized version.

## One hard rule

Some props relate to a real combination or entry code. **Do not write any actual
digits, codes, or code-derived values anywhere in your response**, even if they
appear in your existing files. Describe the field and say it is filled in by
hand. If any of your artwork currently contains real digits, flag that fact
without reproducing them.
